# General Scheduling Philosophy

**Last revised:** November 24, 2026

The recommended scheduling system is organized around how products are:

1. Selected
2. Specified
3. Purchased
4. Installed

The primary principle is:

Maintain one authoritative location for each selection, with other drawings referencing it.

Not every product requires its own schedule, and not every selection needs to appear in the architectural drawing set.

Schedules should remain appropriate to the scale and scope of the residential project.

## Recommended Schedule Conventions

- Use compact uppercase schedule titles and column headings, with grouped headings where useful.
- Maintain consistent typography, table rules, and row spacing.
- Place numbered schedule notes immediately below their schedules; reference them through NOTES columns.
- Use `TBD` for unresolved information and `-` for nonapplicable information.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication between schedules and drawings.
- Favor repeatable product-type identifiers for standard products and unique identifiers for individually configured assemblies.
- Treat provisional schedule columns as templates for refinement, not finalized requirements.
